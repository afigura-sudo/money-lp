Frequently asked questions

Are U.S. News Money rankings independent?

Yes. Our rankings are editorially independent, and advertisers do not decide how products are ordered. We may earn a commission when you apply for a product through our links. Our Advertiser Disclosure and Editorial Guidelines explain how this works.

What is a good credit score?

On the FICO scale of 300 to 850, a score of 670 to 739 is generally considered good, 740 to 799 is very good and 800 or higher is exceptional. Lenders use your score, along with your income and debts, to set your rate and decide whether to approve you.

What is the difference between APR and APY?

APR is the yearly cost of borrowing, including interest and some fees, and applies to loans and credit cards. APY is the yearly return on savings, including the effect of compounding. When you borrow, look for a lower APR. When you save, look for a higher APY.

How much house can I afford?

A common guideline is the 28/36 rule: keep housing costs at or below 28% of your gross monthly income and total debt payments at or below 36%. Use the mortgage calculator above to estimate a monthly payment, then compare rates from several lenders.

How much should I keep in an emergency fund?

Many financial planners suggest saving three to six months of essential expenses in an account you can reach quickly, such as a high-yield savings account. If that feels out of reach, start with a smaller goal, like $1,000, and add to it over time.
